Tactile Genesis

Simulating Tactile Sensors at Scale to Explore Dexterous Task Learning

Anonymous Authors

● Code 📈 Results

Abstract

Tactile sensing is critical for contact-rich dexterous manipulation, yet it remains unclear which tactile abstractions a policy needs and when richer tactile fields justify their hardware cost. This is hard to study empirically: changing sensors can alter hand geometry, compliance, and calibration, while systematic hardware comparisons are costly and difficult to control.

We present Tactile Genesis, a GPU-parallel tactile sensor simulation platform that exposes binary contact, contact depth, per-taxel kinematic force/torque, elastomer marker displacement, and proximity sensing under a common interface, with configurable placement, resolution, and models of common measurement imperfections (drift, hysteresis, dead taxels, crosstalk). It scales past 16,384 parallel environments and 1,000 taxels on a single GPU, improving throughput by 3 to 20 times over previous tactile simulators. Our elastomer model yields lower marker-displacement error than FOTS and HydroShear for both dilation and shear.

We use the platform to compare 8 tactile representations and 3 force/torque imperfection levels across three hand-task pairs. We find that richer force information is most useful for contact-rich dexterous manipulation, while depth/pressure cues are sufficient for grasp-and-lift. Sensitivity to measurement imperfections is likewise task-dependent, showing that tactile sensor design should be matched to the manipulation regime.

Five tactile abstractions and an auxiliary surface-distance query share configurable probe layouts. The eight policy representations use per-taxel fields or compact per-link readouts; a proprioception-only baseline (none) completes the comparison.

Sensors

Six readouts share one probe layout and one configurable interface. A layout is a set of positions, normals and sensing radii on any link of any robot; a readout decides what those probes report. Swapping --sensors=actual-hand/force_torque for .../elastomer changes what the policy feels without touching the hand.

Measurement imperfections

Every readout supports the same imperfections: read delay and jitter, noise, bias, drift, quantization, sensing-radius and gain variation, dead taxels, spatial crosstalk, and single-Maxwell hysteresis. The clean, mild and strong profiles are spelled out in conf/sensor/tactile_params.yaml, and a policy can be trained and evaluated under any of them.

Hands and Layouts

The same readouts attach to any hand. Each hand carries a layout derived from its real device alongside synthetic whole-hand layouts at roughly 128 and 512 probes, so taxel budget is an experimental variable rather than a hardware commitment. Pick a hand and a layout.

Rendered placements

Radii are half the median nearest-neighbour spacing per link: a simulation footprint, not a vendor calibration. Every layout records its region counts, grid shapes and source hashes in layout_metadata.json, and regenerates with scripts/generate_synthetic_tactile.py.

Teacher–Student Distillation

Tactile Representation Study

Three deliberately different hand-task pairs act as design probes: reposing with the Wuji hand, continuous in-finger rotation with Sharpa, and grasp-and-lift with Allegro. Holding the policy, task and hand fixed and varying only the tactile abstraction is what the platform is for; the sweep below is one instance of it.

What it shows is that the useful tactile signal moves with the manipulation regime rather than with the richness of the sensor. Grasp-and-lift is best served by depth cues, reposing by a compact aggregate force readout, and continuous in-finger rotation by taxel-resolved force/torque, while on grasp-and-lift the kinematic force representations fall below the proprioception-only baseline that depth beats. Sensitivity to measurement imperfection is task-dependent in the same way. A single sensing recommendation would not have transferred across these three pairs.

Beyond Contact Mechanics

The temperature sensor returns a degrees-Celsius field over a voxelized link, integrating contact conduction through a harmonic-mean interface conductivity, spectral diffusion, and optional generation and radiation. Material identity is not observable from contact forces alone, but heat flow depends on conductivity and heat capacity, so the two cues are close to independent and combining them is what separates the materials.
